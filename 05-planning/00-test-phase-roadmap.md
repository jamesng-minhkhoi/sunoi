## The bigger picture
The home-kitchen phase was a smoke test for a much larger goal: James and Hien plan to build **a chain of F&B locations**. The strategic driver is scaling fast toward financial freedom, not staying a single shop. That reframes what "success" and "good enough" mean at every stage:
- Ops SOPs should be written so a 3rd person (not James/Hien) could follow them at a future location
- Pricing/margin data should be treated as the baseline economics model for future locations, not a one-off number
- Marketing learnings (which channel, which offer, what converts) should be documented as a repeatable playbook
- Whatever is learned setting up this first real storefront — lease, fit-out, lighting, signage, licensing, staffing — is the template for location #2

## Success criteria (rewritten 2026-09-12, now that rent is known)
Counted from Day 0 = 2026-09-14. The daily fixed nut is **~267,000đ/day** (8,000,000đ/month: 6M rent + 2M assumed utilities), rising to ~300,000đ/day from month 4.

- **Week 2 bar**: consistently ≥ **11–13 orders/day**, with walk-in + direct the majority. That's the break-even line. Equally valid route: fewer orders with **bigger baskets** — 2-cup orders roughly halve the count needed.
- **Week 4 bar**: daily contribution clearing ~267,000đ on most days, and a measurable share of **repeat** customers — first-time traffic from an opening promo proves nothing on its own.
- **Strong result**: 20–30 orders/day with walk-in/direct dominant, clearing fixed costs with room, by the end of the 3-month reduced-rent window.
- **Serious warning sign**: still under ~10 orders/day at week 4 with the promo spent. At that point diagnose honestly whether it's a **demand** problem (nobody knows SUNOI exists) or a **site** problem (the location can't generate footfall — the same question ZEN CAFE's departure raises). Those have very different answers, and the rent is contractual either way.
